The problem

Most of your marketing arrives in Google Analytics unlabelled

When someone taps a link inside the Facebook or Instagram app, an email, a PDF, a text message or a QR code, the website usually can't tell where they came from. Google Analytics files those visits as Direct or Unassigned, which really means "no idea".

The fix is a few labels added to the end of the link, called UTM tags. They tell Google Analytics the source, the type of channel and the campaign, so every visit gets credited to the thing that earned it.

How it works

Anatomy of a tagged link

It's the same page, with labels added after the ?. Visitors land in exactly the same place. The tags are only read by Google Analytics.

https://yourbusiness.com.au/workshops?utm_source=facebook1&utm_medium=social2&utm_campaign=funding_workshop_jun_263&utm_content=reel4
  1. SourceRequiredThe site, platform or publication the visitor came from: facebook, newsletter, flyer.
  2. MediumRequiredThe type of channel: social, email, paid_social, qr. This decides which channel GA4 puts the visit in, so pick it from the list.
  3. CampaignRequiredThe promotion, offer or event, plus the month and year: funding_workshop_jun_26. Use the same name on every link for that campaign.
  4. ContentOptionalWhat tells two links in the same campaign apart: reel vs carousel, header_button vs footer_button.
The habit

Build, copy, paste, every time a link leaves the building

  1. Build

    Open your link builder and pick the shortcut that matches, like "Facebook post" or "Email newsletter". Paste the page and choose or type the campaign.

  2. Copy

    Save it. It's copied and stored in your library. Use a short link for print, QR codes and SMS.

  3. Paste

    Use the tagged link in your post, email, ad or design, never the plain address. Reusing it later? Find it in My links.

The house rules

Consistency is the whole game

Google Analytics treats Facebook, facebook and facebook.com as three different sources. Tiny differences split your results into pieces. The link builder applies these rules for you:

  • Always lowercase. facebook, never Facebook.
  • Underscores, not spaces. spring_sale_oct_26.
  • Pick from the list. Use the standard sources and mediums so GA4 puts visits in the right channel.
  • Reuse campaign names. Every link for one campaign shares one name. The builder warns you about near-misses.
  • Only tag links to your own site from outside it. Never tag links between pages of your own website, because that restarts the visit and wipes out the real source.
